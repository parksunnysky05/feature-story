The Last Card

A Filipino LGBTQ+ Worker’s Quiet Strength Abroad

By Chandyll Park

A Quiet Night After Work

The biting wind of a South Korean winter waits outside, but inside Venn's cramped room, a different kind of endurance begins. He carefully hangs up his heavy factory jacket, the physical residue of a ten-hour shift finally lifting from his shoulders. But the rest is momentary. Before his body can fully relax, a notification light blinks on his phone—a silent signal from his family in the Philippines, thousands of miles away. It is well past midnight, but instead of sleeping, he opens his laptop and logs into his online university class. The transition is immediate and brutal: from manual labor to mental focus, driven by a deep sense of purpose that allows no room for procrastination.

Being the “Last Card”

To his family, Venn is more than someone who sends money home. He describes himself as their “last card”—the final, reliable person they depend on when structural support fails or crises emerge. Each role he takes on—breadwinner, factory hand, and student—carries overlapping responsibilities. As Venn reflects, his role pushes him to be careful: "I always ask myself if what I’m doing will bring me closer to a better future... It pushes me to stay focused, work hard, and avoid choices that might waste the opportunities I have."

“Being the ‘last card’ in my family means I feel a deep responsibility to succeed, not only for myself but also for them.”

Working in a Male-Dominated Space

Most of Venn’s days are consumed by the repetitive, grueling rhythm of a factory floor—an environment often defined by conservative attitudes and rigid expectations of masculinity. As a gay Filipino worker in South Korea, navigating this space means finding a delicate, unspoken balance: performing high-level professional work while carefully managing how much of his true self he reveals. He lets his actions define him rather than his orientation, focusing on doing his job well and treating everyone with respect. Over time, this deliberate strategy has yielded results: people have started to see him for his work ethic and character rather than his identity.

“In my workplace, men are often expected to be tough, quiet about their emotions, and always strong.”

Life Beyond the Factory

While factory life demands physical endurance, Venn transitions at night to coursework requiring mental focus. This dual burden is exhausting, yet necessary. This quiet perseverance is further contrasted on the volleyball court, where Venn is a competitive athlete. In an arena often synonymous with aggressive performance, he uses his physical skill and control to earn respect on his own terms. For Venn, playing volleyball is a space where he can fully express his passion, even if it challenges others’ assumptions.

“When you perform well and contribute to the team, people respect you for your skill and commitment.”

Quiet Strength

Ultimately, Venn returns to the same quiet room where his relentless cycle begins. Before resting his physically and mentally exhausted body, he completes one final, critical transaction: sending remittances back to his family in the Philippines. This daily, deliberate act of love and duty is the true heart of his story. It challenges the conventional idea that ‘masculine strength’ must always be loud, ambitious, and dominant. Venn shows us that true resilience is often steady, responsible, and quietly persistent—a powerful form of resistance from a 'last card' defying all odds.

“For me, strength is not always loud or visible. Sometimes strength is simply continuing to move forward even when things are difficult.”

Interview Source & Ethical Consent

This feature story is based on a structured personal interview conducted verbatim with Venn in **February 2026**. Venn provided full permission and informed consent for the use of his actual name and story.
