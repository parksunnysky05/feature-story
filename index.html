The Last Card: A Filipino LGBTQ+ Worker’s Quiet Strength Abroad

By Chandyll Park

Venn, a Filipino migrant worker in South Korea balancing work, study, and family responsibility.

A Quiet Night After Work

The winter air in South Korea cuts sharply after a long factory shift ends. Venn steps into his small, silent room and meticulously removes his heavy winter jacket, placing it carefully beside his bed. He does not rest immediately. A message from his family in the Philippines blinks on his smartphone screen, a quiet reminder of home. He glances at it briefly, exhales softly, and opens his laptop.

It is well past midnight, but he must log into his online university class. Balancing the exhaustion of physical manufacturing with the mental discipline required for university coursework is perhaps his greatest daily challenge. 'It’s a shift that never ends,' he says, yet this education is the foundation he is building—not just for himself, but for his family's long-term future.

After long factory shifts, Venn continues attending his university classes late at night.

Being the “Last Card”

To his family, Venn is more than someone who sends money home. He describes himself as their “last card”—the person they rely on when things become difficult.

“Being the ‘last card’ in my family means I feel a deep responsibility to succeed, not only for myself but also for them.”

Like many Filipino migrant workers, Venn regularly sends remittances home.

Working in a Male-Dominated Space

Most of Venn’s days are consumed by the repetitive, grueling rhythm of a factory floor—an environment often defined by conservative attitudes and rigid expectations of masculinity. As a gay Filipino worker in South Korea, navigating this space means finding a delicate, unspoken balance: performing high-level professional work while carefully managing how much of his true self he reveals, letting his competence overwrite potential prejudice.

“In my workplace, men are often expected to be tough, quiet about their emotions, and always strong.”

Venn works long shifts in a male-dominated factory environment.

Life Beyond the Factory

Venn playing competitive volleyball.

Outside of work and school, Venn finds another space where he can express himself—on the volleyball court. In a arena often synonymous with aggressive performance, he uses his physical skill and control to earn respect on his own terms.

“When you perform well and contribute to the team, people respect you for your skill and commitment.”

Quiet Strength

Ultimately, Venn returns to the same quiet room where his relentless cycle begins. Before resting his physically and mentally exhausted body, he completes one final, critical transaction: sending remittances back to his family in the Philippines. This daily, deliberate act of love and duty is the true heart of his story. It challenges the conventional idea that ‘masculine strength’ must always be loud, ambitious, and dominant. Venn shows us that true resilience is often steady, responsible, and quietly persistent—a powerful form of resistance from a 'last card' defying all odds.

“For me, strength is not always loud or visible. Sometimes strength is simply continuing to move forward even when things are difficult.”

Despite the challenges of working abroad, Venn continues building a future for himself and his family.
