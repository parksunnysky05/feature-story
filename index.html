The Last Card: A Filipino LGBTQ+ Worker’s Quiet Strength Abroad

By Chandyll Park

Venn, a Filipino migrant worker in South Korea balancing work, study, and family responsibility.

A Quiet Night After Work

The winter air in South Korea cuts sharply after a long factory shift ends. Venn steps into his small, silent room and removes his heavy winter jacket, placing it carefully beside his bed.

He does not rest immediately. A message from his family in the Philippines blinks on his smartphone screen, a quiet reminder of home. He glances at it briefly, exhales softly, and opens his laptop.

It is well past midnight, but he logs into his online university class.

After long factory shifts, Venn continues attending his university classes late at night.

Being the “Last Card”

To his family, Venn is more than someone who sends money home. He describes himself as their “last card”—the person they rely on when things become difficult.

“Being the ‘last card’ in my family means I feel a deep responsibility to succeed, not only for myself but also for them.”

Like many Filipino migrant workers, Venn regularly sends remittances home.

Working in a Male-Dominated Space

Most of Venn’s days are spent inside a factory where long shifts are common and traditional expectations of masculinity remain strong.

“In my workplace, men are often expected to be tough, quiet about their emotions, and always strong.”

Venn works long shifts in a male-dominated factory environment.

Life Beyond the Factory

Venn playing competitive volleyball.

Outside of work and school, Venn finds another space where he can express himself—on the volleyball court.

“When you perform well and contribute to the team, people respect you for your skill and commitment.”

Quiet Strength

At the end of the day, Venn returns to the same quiet room where his routine began. Before sleeping, he sends money home to his family in the Philippines.

“For me, strength is not always loud or visible. Sometimes strength is simply continuing to move forward even when things are difficult.”

Despite the challenges of working abroad, Venn continues building a future for himself and his family.
